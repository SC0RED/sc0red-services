# Vector Advisory Strategy-Map House Style

This guide defines the distinctive house style for Vector Advisory
strategy maps, derived from a 2011 client engagement deck (Wawa). The
style sits ATOP the Kaplan & Norton framework defined in
`kaplan_norton_framework.md`. Every generated map MUST follow these
conventions.

## Customer perspective: first-person customer voice

Customer-perspective objective titles MUST be written as quoted
first-person statements, AS IF THE CUSTOMER IS SPEAKING. Not
corporate-voice imperatives. Not third-person descriptions.

✓ GOOD:
- *"Offer fresh and inviting food and beverages that meet my needs in a pleasant environment."*
- *"Provide me with the products I need, value I expect, and fast service in convenient locations."*
- *"Wawa associates show they really care about me."*

✗ BAD:
- "Increase customer satisfaction" (corporate, generic)
- "Serve fresh food" (imperative, not customer voice)
- "Customers value fresh products" (third person)

The objective DEFINITION (the 50-150 word paragraph below the title)
also continues in first-person customer voice:

> "Wawa is my preferred destination for on-the-go breakfast,
> beverages, lunch, dinner and snacks. I choose Wawa because of the
> signature products, excellent in-store shopping experience and a
> store that looks good inside and out. I am a fan of the brand, and
> passionately recommend Wawa to others."

## Internal Processes: organised into 2-3 named themes

Internal Processes objectives MUST NOT appear as a flat list. They
MUST be grouped into 2-3 strategic themes, each with a named header.
The themes connect directly to the Financial perspective's revenue or
productivity strategies — readers should be able to trace each theme
to a financial outcome it supports.

Example structure (from Wawa):

```
Theme 1 — "Grow Through Foodservice"
   I1. Develop fresh food and beverage offers that satisfy needs across all day parts
   I2. Clearly communicate our "fresh and inviting" message to our customers
   I3. Create an appealing store facility and environment
   I4. Create an enjoyable, friendly and customer-focused store environment

Theme 2 — "Deliver Convenience and Value"
   I5. Continuously improve the throughput, quality, and cost of our end-to-end processes
   I6. Protect and grow customer count through our fuel, convenience and service offers

Theme 3 — "Expand Profitably"
   I7. Develop a range of appetizing, efficient, and flexible store prototypes and remodel designs
   I8. Generate a high quantity of high-quality sites that fulfill strategic market plans
   I9. Continuously refine the design and construction process to extract cost
```

Theme names MUST be verb-led (start with a verb or gerund). They are
shorter and punchier than objective titles — typically 2-5 words.

## Organizational Capacity (renamed from "Learning and Growth")

Vector renames the canonical "Learning and Growth" perspective to
**Organizational Capacity**. The internal triad is exactly three
objectives, one each:

- **People** — talent, skills, leadership, culture-of-ownership
- **Technology** — systems, data, tools, technical capability
- **Culture** — values, ways of working, organisational alignment

(Note: the leader's 2011 Wawa engagement used "Financial Management"
as the third bucket. For Vector v1, we use **Culture** instead —
canonical to K&N and more universal across industries. Financial
Management would be specific to privately-held employee-owned cases
and is not generic enough.)

## Vision: quoted, at the top

Vision goes at the very top of the map, in quote marks, attributed
to the company. Example:

> *"To be the world's most appetizing convenience retailer."*

If the company has not published an explicit vision statement,
synthesise one from public materials (about-us page, investor
relations copy, press releases) and mark with `(synthesised from
public materials)` after the quote. Synthesised visions become
candidate gaps for the "What's Missing?" panel.

## Mission: one sentence below the vision

Mission states the current-tense purpose. Concise. Plain language.

✓ GOOD: "Provide convenient, high-quality food, beverages, and fuel
to commuters in the U.S. mid-Atlantic region."

✗ BAD: "We strive to be the leading provider of best-in-class
solutions that delight our valued customers." (vague, vanity)

## Strategic Priorities: 3 themed columns above the map

The header band above the map shows 3 strategic priorities
(corresponding to the Internal Processes themes) and a one-line
"strategic result" each — what success looks like for that priority.

Example:

```
Strategic Priorities:    Grow Through    Deliver Convenience    Expand
                         Foodservice     and Value              Profitably
Strategic Results:       Best-in-class   Industry-leading       50% store
                         signature       value perception       footprint
                         food platform                          growth
```

The strategic priorities map 1:1 to the Internal Processes themes.
Generate them once and reuse the names; this is not duplicate work.

## Objective definitions: 50–150 words, "We will…" voice

Every objective (except Customer perspective, which uses customer
voice) has a definition paragraph. Length: 50-150 words. First-person
plural voice ("We will...", "Our..."). Where possible, name specific
real or candidate initiatives.

✓ GOOD (190 words, slightly long but acceptable):

> "We will deliver strong talent development processes to ensure that
> associates are engaged and have the skills, experiences and
> competencies to deliver an outstanding customer experience resulting
> in a financially successful company. We will develop a comprehensive
> plan for recruitment, learning & development, retention, leadership
> development and succession to ensure a deep, diverse and robust
> talent pipeline, committed to the principles of servant leadership
> to deliver the brand experience throughout the organization."

✗ BAD:

> "Improve employee engagement." (no rationale, no specifics, too
> short)

Definitions in our public-data v1 may be SHORTER than full Vector
consulting deliverables — public data won't always support 150-word
specifics. Aim for 50-100 words when grounding is thin; 100-150 when
the public data is rich. NEVER under 30 words.

## Connector phrases: narrative arrows between perspectives

Between each pair of perspectives in the visual layout, include a
narrative connector phrase. These phrases turn the diagram from
"boxes and arrows" into a readable story:

```
Live our values
       ↓
Organizational Capacity perspective
       ↓ "Enables us to deliver"
Internal Processes perspective
       ↓ "Which simplify the lives of our..."
Customer perspective
       ↓ "Who reward us with..."
Financial perspective
```

Use these exact phrases (or close variants) so generated maps are
recognisable as Vector-style.

## Core values strip at the bottom

Below the Organizational Capacity perspective, render a single-line
core values statement. Example: "Live our values: value people,
delight customers, embrace change, do the right thing, do things
right, passion for winning."

If the company has published values explicitly (about-us page,
careers page, "what we stand for" sections), use those. If it
hasn't, synthesise 4-6 values from public materials and mark
`(inferred)`. Inferred values become candidate gaps.

## What we DO NOT include in v1

- **No measures**, **no targets**, **no initiatives**. These are deep-
  dive deliverables. The strategy map for v1 stops at vision +
  mission + priorities + objectives + definitions + arrows + gaps.
  This boundary is not a limitation — it is the conversion mechanic.
  The visible absence of those columns is what makes the deep-dive
  CTA specific.
- **No company-confidential claims**. Public data only. If something
  isn't in the company's public materials or doesn't follow from a
  defensible industry pattern, it doesn't go in the map. Every
  objective carries a confidence marker (HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW) so the
  reader can see what's grounded vs. inferred.
- **No generic objective titles** ("Improve customer satisfaction",
  "Drive operational excellence"). Every objective MUST be specific
  to this company's situation, even at the cost of the objective
  being more provisional. Generic objectives are the K&N
  KPI-illusion anti-pattern (see `anti_patterns.md`).

## Confidence markers — one per objective

Every objective is tagged with HIGH / MEDIUM / LOW confidence:

- **HIGH** — directly inferred from concrete public data (e.g.
  financial objective derived from EBITDA tree numerical estimates).
- **MEDIUM** — typical of similar companies in this industry; pattern-
  matched but not directly observed.
- **LOW** — inferred from absence; reasonable but unverified. LOW-
  confidence objectives are natural candidates for the "What's
  Missing?" panel.

The frontend renders the marker as a small chip on each objective
card. The presence of confidence markers is part of the credibility:
prospects can see we're being honest about what's grounded.
