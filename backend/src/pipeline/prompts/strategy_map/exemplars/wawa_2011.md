# Wawa Company-Level Strategy Map — 2011

**Industry**: convenience retail, food service, fuel
**Source**: leader's 2011 client engagement (provided as reference
material for sc0red Advisory v1)
**Why included**: this is the canonical Vector house-style example.
Demonstrates customer-voice quotes, themed Internal Processes,
"We will…" definitions, the People & Organization perspective with
the People / Technology / Culture triad (Wawa's original used
Financial Management; Vector v1 uses Culture instead — see
`sc0red_advisory_style_guide.md`).

When generating new strategy maps, use this exemplar as the primary
style reference — its tone, structure, and depth set the bar for
what Vector-style output looks like.

## Vision

*"To be the world's most appetizing convenience retailer."*

## Mission

(Implicit in the original deck — synthesise as: "Provide an
appetizing, convenient food, beverage, and fuel experience in the
U.S. mid-Atlantic.")

## Customer Value Proposition

**Customer Intimacy** (with Operational Excellence support).

Wawa positions on emotional connection — friendly associates,
community presence, trusted brand — while keeping the convenience-
store and fuel operations efficient enough to compete with discount
operators.

## Strategic Priorities

These map 1:1 to the Internal Processes themes below.

| Priority | Strategic Result |
|---|---|
| Grow Through Foodservice | Best-in-class signature food and beverage platform across all day parts |
| Deliver Convenience and Value | Industry-leading customer perception of speed and value |
| Expand Profitably | High-quantity, high-quality store growth in core and new markets |

## Financial Perspective (4 objectives)

**F1. Grow Net Income by average annual rate of 15% through 2020.**

> "Profitable growth provides opportunities for our associates,
> ensures fair returns to our owners and enables us to accomplish our
> strategic plan and core purpose. Targeting annual growth of 15% of
> Net Income while entering a new market and reinvesting in existing
> markets is aggressive and can only be achieved by excelling in our
> 3 areas of business — foodservice, fuel and convenience."

**F2. Grow revenue and customer count in all existing stores and
markets.**

> "There is an opportunity to grow same-store sales by further
> developing our fresh food and beverage experience while maintaining
> fuel and customer count transactions. We will know we have been
> successful when we attract new customers, additional visits from
> current customers, and increased basket size."

**F3. Operate efficiently and optimize allocation of resources.**

> "An efficient cost structure and processes are an important part of
> delivering our value proposition to the customer and fair returns
> to our shareholders. We will drive efficiency by: (1) continuing
> to enable, automate and streamline processes and technologies; and
> (2) allocating resources, human and financial, to deliver the
> maximum returns on investment."

**F4. Grow profitably through new stores and markets.**

> "To achieve our desired growth goals we must open profitable new
> stores, in both new and existing markets. Success will come through
> adapting to market-specific customer needs, effective store design,
> strategic market strategies, a high quantity of high-quality sites,
> and a continuous focus on build cost."

## Customer Perspective (4 objectives — first-person customer voice)

**C1. *"Offer fresh and inviting food and beverages that meet my needs
in a pleasant environment."***

> "Wawa is my preferred destination for on-the-go breakfast,
> beverages, lunch, dinner and snacks. I choose Wawa because of the
> signature products, excellent in-store shopping experience and a
> store that looks good inside and out. I am a fan of the brand, and
> passionately recommend Wawa to others."

**C2. *"Provide me with the products I need, value I expect, and fast
service in convenient locations."***

> "Wawa stores are located conveniently to me. Wawa provides me with
> strong value by offering the products and services that simplify
> my shopping experience. I appreciate the quick shopping visit
> since I am starved for time."

**C3. *"Wawa associates show they really care about me."***

> "Shopping at Wawa is a pleasure. I am served by friendly,
> knowledgeable associates who show they really care about their
> customers and fulfilling my needs is their primary focus."

**C4. *"Wawa is a good neighbor with strong values and a culture that
supports community efforts."***

> "Wawa supports communities and customers in many different ways and
> is a company with strong values and a belief in social
> responsibility. Whether it is through charity events, support of
> local organizations or by promoting sustainability, Wawa can be
> counted on to make the community a better place to live and work."

## Internal Processes Perspective (9 objectives in 3 themes)

### Theme 1 — "Grow Through Foodservice"

**I1. Develop fresh food and beverage offers that satisfy needs across
all day parts.**

> "We have made great strides in developing a compelling, quality food
> and beverage offer that represents value to our customers. We must
> now create and improve the offer to meet customer needs across all
> day parts, especially dinner. We will protect and grow core
> categories by staying abreast of market and consumer trends,
> innovating products and platforms, and creating signature offerings."

**I2. Clearly communicate our "fresh and inviting" message to our
customers.**

> "We must communicate that our offer is fresh and inviting through
> effective channels, messaging and timing of communications to
> increase customer count and loyalty. We must develop and communicate
> our brand identity and leverage technology to create a more
> personalized experience with our customers."

**I3. Create an appealing store facility and environment.**

> "We must create a fresh and inviting store atmosphere that appeals
> to the senses and creates a great brand experience. Basic
> environmental standards like cleanliness are executed flawlessly,
> which allows the brand experience to communicate that we 'give
> care' to our offer."

**I4. Create an enjoyable, friendly and customer-focused store
environment for our associates that drives consumer trial and brand
loyalty.**

> "Customer-focused associates are fundamental in delivering on our
> fresh and inviting brand promise, and building loyalty in new and
> existing customer segments. Our friendly associates will create a
> compelling in-store experience by engaging customers at key points
> of interaction and demonstrating knowledge about our products."

### Theme 2 — "Deliver Convenience and Value"

**I5. Continuously improve the throughput, quality, and cost of our
end-to-end processes.**

> "A disciplined, cross-functional approach to business processes
> defines how we work and creates value for Wawa. We will use a
> data-driven approach to proactively identify and pursue the biggest
> process opportunities to reduce costs and deliver enhanced value to
> our customers."

**I6. Protect and grow customer count through our fuel, convenience
and service offers.**

> "We must continue to protect and grow our leading market share in
> fuel and key convenience drivers. This requires an on-going effort
> to edit and enhance our existing offers and explore new offers to
> satisfy the needs of our customers."

### Theme 3 — "Expand Profitably"

**I7. Develop a range of appetizing, efficient, and flexible store
prototypes and remodel designs.**

> "A range of fuel store prototypes will enable us to capitalize on
> real estate market opportunities with appropriate prototypes. Store
> designs will reflect the current best thinking to deliver an
> appetizing offer."

**I8. Generate a high quantity of high-quality sites that fulfill
strategic market plans.**

> "We must generate a high quantity of new stores to deliver on our
> growth plans. For both core and new markets, we will create
> strategic market plans by geography. Our aggressive pipeline targets
> will be designed to meet the required quantities specified by our
> strategic market plans."

**I9. Continuously refine the design and construction process to
extract cost.**

> "In order to meet store profitability targets, it is critical to
> control store investment costs for both new stores and remodels.
> We will have effective construction bidding processes, real estate
> procurement flexibility, and design specifications."

## Organizational Capacity Perspective (3 objectives — People /
Technology / Culture)

**Note**: the original 2011 Wawa deck used "Financial Management" as
the third bucket. For sc0red Advisory v1, we use **Culture** instead
(canonical to K&N, more universal). The example below shows what a
Culture objective for Wawa would look like to fit Vector v1 style.

**P1 (PEOPLE) — Invest in and develop our associates to create high
engagement and a culture of ownership.**

> "We will deliver strong talent development processes to ensure that
> associates are engaged and have the skills, experiences and
> competencies to deliver an outstanding customer experience. We will
> develop a comprehensive plan for recruitment, learning and
> development, retention, leadership development and succession to
> ensure a deep, diverse and robust talent pipeline."

**P2 (TECHNOLOGY) — Deliver reliable technologies and support
services, valuable insight, and innovative solutions.**

> "We will provide consistently reliable technical products and
> support services, valuable insights to make forward-looking
> business decisions, and cost effective and innovative solutions.
> To deliver this, we will drive a process-centric approach that
> ensures the highest return on technology investments."

**P3 (CULTURE) — Live our values to create the foundation for our
strategy.** *(Vector v1 substitution)*

> "Our values — value people, delight customers, embrace change, do
> the right thing, do things right, and a passion for winning — are
> the foundation of how we work, how we serve customers, and how we
> grow. We will live these values consistently across the
> organization, embedding them in hiring, performance management,
> recognition, and decision-making, so that they are visible in
> every interaction with associates, customers, and the communities
> we serve."

## Causal Narrative

```
Live our values
       ↓
Organizational Capacity perspective (People / Technology / Culture)
       ↓ "Enables us to deliver"
Internal Processes perspective (the 3 themes)
       ↓ "Which simplify the lives of our..."
Customer perspective (the 4 quoted objectives)
       ↓ "Who reward us with..."
Financial perspective (F1-F4)
```

## Core Values (foundation strip at the bottom)

> Live our values: value people, delight customers, embrace change,
> do the right thing, do things right, passion for winning.

## Annotations: what to mimic in generated maps

- **Customer perspective is in first-person customer voice with
  quotation marks.** Every C-objective is a quote.
- **Internal Processes are organised into 2-3 named themes.** Theme
  names are verb-led (Grow…, Deliver…, Expand…). Themes connect
  to revenue strategies in the Financial perspective.
- **Definitions are 50-150 words in "We will…" voice.** They name
  specific real or candidate initiatives where possible.
- **Organizational Capacity has exactly 3 objectives**, one for each
  bucket: People / Technology / Culture (Vector v1 — note this
  differs from the original Wawa deck which used Financial Management).
- **Vision is quoted at the top.** Core values strip at the bottom.
- **Connector phrases between perspectives** turn the diagram into a
  story.

## What NOT to mimic from this exemplar

- **The 9 Internal Process objectives**. That's a lot. Wawa got that
  count because it was a paid consulting engagement with deep access.
  Public-data v1 typically supports 4-6 Internal Process objectives —
  fewer if data is sparse. Don't pad.
- **Wawa's "Financial Management" P-perspective bucket**. Use Culture
  for v1 (see `sc0red_advisory_style_guide.md` and the framework guide).
- **The 50-150 word definitions at the high end of the range**.
  Public-data definitions will often be 50-100 words because the
  ground truth is thinner. That's fine — quality over length.
