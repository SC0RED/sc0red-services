# Mobil North American Marketing & Refining — 2000

**Industry**: petroleum refining and marketing (commodity products,
gasoline + non-gasoline)
**Source**: Kaplan & Norton, HBR September–October 2000
**Result**: operating cash flow improved by more than $1 billion per
year; ROCE grew from 6% to 16%; sales growth exceeded industry
average by more than 2% annually; cash expenses decreased by 20%.
**Why included**: the canonical example of a HYBRID value-proposition
case (customer intimacy + operational excellence). Demonstrates the
two-column financial perspective (revenue growth + productivity) and
extensive cause-and-effect arrows.

## Vision

*"To be the best integrated refiner-marketer in the United States by
efficiently delivering unprecedented value to customers."*

## Mission

Mobil sought to transform itself from a centrally-controlled
manufacturer of commodity products into a decentralized, customer-
driven organization.

## Customer Value Proposition

**Hybrid: Customer Intimacy + Operational Excellence**

Mobil targeted premium customer segments by offering them a great
buying experience (customer intimacy) while keeping operating costs
per gallon at the lowest level in the industry (operational
excellence). This is the canonical hybrid case in the K&N literature —
neither single proposition fits.

Rationale: Mobil discovered through research that price-sensitive
consumers represented only ~20% of gasoline purchasers, while ~60%
were willing to pay significant price premiums for fast, friendly,
well-outfitted stations. This shifted the strategy toward the premium-
experience customers (intimacy) but only viable if operating costs
stayed at industry-leading lows (excellence).

## Strategic Priorities

1. **Differentiate on Customer Buying Experience** — fast, friendly,
   well-outfitted stations targeting premium segments.
2. **Reduce Total Delivered Cost** — refinery yield, asset
   utilization, supply chain efficiency.
3. **Win-Win Dealer Relations** — dealers as partners, not just
   distribution.

## Financial Perspective (4 objectives in 2 columns)

### Revenue Growth Strategy ("understand customers' needs and differentiate accordingly")

**F1. Introduce new sources of nongasoline revenue through expanded
convenience store presence.**

   Mobil grew revenue by selling more non-gasoline products and
   services — convenience store products, ancillary automotive services
   (car washes, oil changes, minor repairs), automotive products (oil,
   antifreeze, wiper fluid), and common replacement parts (tires and
   wiper blades).

**F2. Sell more premium brands to increase customer profitability.**

   Mobil increased sales of premium-brand gasoline at higher margins,
   targeting the ~60% of consumers willing to pay premiums for a
   superior buying experience.

### Productivity Growth Strategy ("maximize use of existing assets; integrate the business to reduce total delivered cost")

**F3. Become the industry cost leader in every supply chain category.**

   Slashed operating expenses per gallon to the lowest level in the
   industry. Reduced refinery downtime, increased yields, optimised
   logistics.

**F4. Maximize the use of existing assets.**

   Improved asset utilisation; cash flow versus business plan as the
   primary measure.

## Customer Perspective (Customer Intimacy panel + dealer panel)

### Consumer Customer Intimacy

Product/Service Attributes (✓ for general, gray box for differentiator):
- ✓ clean, ✓ safe, gray "quality product", gray "speedy purchase"

Relationship: friendly helpful workers; recognize customer loyalty.
Image: trusted brand.

Combined goal: **Delight the consumer.** Measures: share of targeted
customer segments; mystery-shopper rating.

### Win-Win Dealer Relations

- Offer more consumer products
- Help dealers develop their business skills

Measures: dealer profitability; dealer satisfaction.

## Internal Processes Perspective (4 objectives across the four
process categories)

(Mobil's I perspective is FLAT in the HBR exhibit — not themed like
Vector's Wawa style. This is one of the conventions that differs
between K&N canonical and Vector house style. When generating Vector-
style maps, themed grouping is preferred.)

### Innovation processes ("build the franchise")

**I1. Create non-gasoline products and services.**

   Develop new product lines in the convenience-store and automotive-
   services categories. Measures: new product acceptance rate; new
   product return on investment.

### Customer-management processes ("increase customer value")

**I2. Understand customer segments better and build best-in-class
franchise teams.**

   Deep customer-segmentation research; equip franchised dealers to
   serve premium-segment consumers. Measures: share of target market;
   dealer quality rating.

### Operations processes ("achieve operational excellence")

**I3. Improve hardware performance and inventory management; deliver
products on spec and on time; become the industry cost leader.**

   Refinery yield gap; unplanned downtime; inventory levels; stockout
   rate; activity-based costs vs. competition.

### Citizenship processes ("be a good corporate citizen")

**I4. Improve environmental health and safety.**

   Reduced number of environmental incidents and safety incidents.

## Learning and Growth Perspective (3 objectives)

(K&N use "Learning and Growth"; Vector renames to "Organizational
Capacity". Same structure either way.)

### Skills / People

**L1. Promote functional excellence; develop leadership skills; create
an integrated view of the company among employees.**

   Measure: ratio of strategic skills to job coverage.

### Technology

**L2. Adopt new technology that encourages and aids process
improvements.**

   Measure: on-time deployment of systems.

### Culture

**L3. Align business and personal goals.**

   Measures: personal balanced scorecards; employee feedback.

## Causal Arrows (linked hypotheses)

Key arrows running upward through the perspectives:

- **L1 (functional excellence) → I3 (operational improvement)**:
  better-skilled employees execute operations better.
- **L2 (technology) → I3 (process improvement)**: deploying new
  monitoring tech reduces refinery downtime.
- **L3 (aligned goals) → I2 (customer-management) → C (delighted
  consumer)**: aligned employees serve customers better.
- **I1 (new products) → C (delighted consumer) → F2 (premium-brand
  sales)**: convenience-store offerings drive premium customer
  loyalty drives margin.
- **I2 (dealer engagement) → C (win-win dealer relations) → F1
  (non-gasoline revenue)**: trained dealers grow non-gasoline
  category sales.
- **I3 (cost leadership) → F3 (cost-leader financial) → F4 (asset
  utilization)**: process improvements directly hit cost objectives.
- **I4 (safety) → I3 (operational excellence)**: safer operations are
  more efficient operations (this is a within-perspective arrow that
  K&N implies).

## "What's Missing?" — example gap diagnostic from this map

Per HBR, when senior management built business-unit-level versions of
this corporate map, they discovered:

1. **One business unit had NO objectives or metrics for dealers** —
   suggesting that unit had either bypassed dealers (selling direct?)
   or considered dealer relationships non-strategic. The gap exposed
   a real strategic question.

2. **Another business unit had NO measure for quality** — suggesting
   either neglect of quality or excessive confidence. Either way, a
   strategic blind spot.

These gaps are the model for our "What's Missing?" generation step.

## Annotations: what to mimic in generated maps

- **Hybrid value proposition** is acceptable when warranted; name
  both propositions and the rationale.
- **Two-column Financial perspective** — Revenue Growth | Productivity
  — is a common pattern, not an obligation. Use when the company has
  distinct revenue-side and cost-side objectives.
- **Multiple Customer-perspective panels** when the company has
  distinct customer types (consumers + dealers in Mobil's case;
  hosts + guests in marketplace cases).
- **Causal arrows are detailed and explicit** — the strength of this
  exemplar is the density of arrows. Generated maps should have at
  least 5 arrows; 8-12 is better.

## What NOT to mimic

- **Flat Internal Processes** — Vector style requires themed grouping.
- **Capacity is "Learning and Growth"** — Vector style renames to
  "Organizational Capacity."
- **No customer-voice quotes in Customer perspective** — Vector style
  requires first-person customer voice. Mobil's Customer perspective
  is described in third-person; we don't follow that here.
